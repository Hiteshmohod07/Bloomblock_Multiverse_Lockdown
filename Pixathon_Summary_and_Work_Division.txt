SUMMARY

Pixathon is a puzzle-building hackathon. Your team must create a playable puzzle game, demonstrate how AI helped, and deliver a 60-second pitch. The four classes in the PDF are team roles: Driver, Artist, Puzzle Master, and Bard.

- Event: 23 October 2026, starting at 3:30 PM at VYAS, MIT-WPU.
- Problem statement: Revealed at the event. Puzzle examples in the slides are illustrations, not the final assignment.
- Submission: A public GitHub repository link is required. A live playable link is optional.
- AI evidence: Include AI_LOG.md with the tools used, 5-10 key prompts, and one example of AI making a mistake and how you corrected it.
- Progress evidence: Commit after each working version.
- Pitch: 60 seconds, with approximately 30 seconds explaining your AI use.
- Build rule: Never go more than 20 minutes without a playable build.

JUDGING CRITERIA

Smart use of AI: 40 points
Evidence that you used AI thoughtfully and checked its results.

Creativity and originality: 20 points
A fresh twist on a puzzle.

Usability: 15 points
Someone unfamiliar with the game can play without help.

Feel and design: 10 points
Consistent visuals, sound, feedback, and polish.

Pitch: 10 points
A clear, convincing demonstration.

Fun factor: 5 points
A game that makes judges want another level.

Total: 100 points.

AI documentation deserves continuous attention because it supports the largest scoring category. The remaining 60 points reward the game and its presentation.

WORK DIVISION BY CLASS

The four classes come from page 19. Their responsibilities are stated in the PDF; the detailed assignments and deliverables below are a suggested implementation plan.

1. DRIVER - CODE AND WORKING BUILD

Main responsibility: Own the code and working build.

Assigned work:
- Set up the public GitHub repository.
- Operate the AI coding tool.
- Implement movement, puzzle rules, win conditions, restart, and level transitions.
- Integrate assets from the Artist and levels from the Puzzle Master.
- Fix bugs and commit working versions.

Deliverables: Playable game, integrated final build, and public repository.

2. ARTIST - VISUALS AND AUDIO

Main responsibility: Own the game's visual and audio presentation.

Assigned work:
- Establish a consistent style.
- Create sprites, tiles, buttons, menus, and gameplay UI.
- Generate music and sound effects.
- Give the Driver assets early and improve them as the game develops.

Deliverables: Organized visual/audio assets, readable UI, and consistent presentation.

3. PUZZLE MASTER - LEVELS, TESTING, AND FUN

Main responsibility: Own level design, testing, and player experience.

Assigned work:
- Define the puzzle rules with the team.
- Design 5 or more levels, each introducing an idea.
- Check that every level is solvable.
- Test controls and edge cases.
- Record bugs and identify confusing or uninteresting gameplay.
- Observe an unfamiliar player testing the game.

Deliverables: Tested level set, solution checks, and prioritized bug reports.

4. BARD - DOCUMENTATION, PITCH, AND TIME

Main responsibility: Own AI_LOG.md, the pitch, and timekeeping.

Assigned work:
- Maintain AI_LOG.md throughout the event.
- Collect prompts, tools used, and AI corrections from everyone.
- Track milestones and remind teammates of deadlines.
- Prepare the demonstration and rehearse the pitch.
- Check submission readiness.

Deliverables: Complete AI log, 60-second pitch, and submission checklist.

TOOLS PRESENTED IN THE PDF

- Driver: Antigravity, Kiro, or Codex for development; v0 or Replit where useful.
- Artist: Stitch for UI, PixelLab for pixel art, Suno for music, and ElevenLabs for sound effects.
- Puzzle Master: Work with the Driver to use AI for level validation or a solver.
- Bard: Collect evidence from every tool used across the team.

These are tools presented in the PDF. You do not need to use every tool; the rubric rewards how well AI is used.

EVENT TIMELINE AND WORK ALLOCATION

The following milestones follow page 18.

3:30-4:00 PM - CHOOSE THE CONCEPT
- Everyone brainstorms.
- Puzzle Master defines the rules.
- Driver checks technical feasibility.
- Artist sets the visual style.
- Bard records decisions.

4:00-4:20 PM - MAKE LEVEL 1 PLAYABLE
- Driver builds the core gameplay.
- Puzzle Master tests it.
- Artist supplies basic assets.
- Bard logs AI use.

4:20-5:10 PM - BUILD LEVELS AND CONTENT
- Puzzle Master creates 5 or more levels.
- Driver integrates and validates them.
- Artist develops assets.
- Bard gathers evidence.

5:10-5:35 PM - POLISH
- Artist leads visuals and audio.
- Driver integrates the improvements.
- Puzzle Master checks clarity and fun.
- Bard drafts the pitch.

5:35-5:50 PM - FINAL TESTING AND RELEASE PREPARATION
- Driver checks the repository and deploys if chosen.
- Puzzle Master observes an unfamiliar player.
- Artist fixes readability issues.
- Bard checks submission materials.

5:50-6:00 PM - PREPARE THE PITCH
- Bard leads rehearsal.
- Driver prepares the demo.
- Everyone supplies their strongest contribution.

Timing discrepancy: The slides call this a "2-hour" plan, but the displayed schedule spans 3:30-6:00 PM, or 2.5 hours. Use the displayed milestones for planning and confirm the actual cutoff with organizers.

SMALLER TEAMS

The PDF allows combining classes. Suggested combinations:
- 3 people: Driver; Artist; Puzzle Master + Bard.
- 2 people: Driver + Bard; Artist + Puzzle Master.
- 1 person: Cover all four roles, keeping the game small enough to finish.
